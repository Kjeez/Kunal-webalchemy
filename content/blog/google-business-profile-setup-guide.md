Short answer: create a free **Google Business Profile** at business.google.com, choose the right category, add your address or service area, verify it, then keep it complete and active with photos, posts and reviews. Google says local results are based on three things: **relevance, distance and prominence**.

For many local businesses, the Maps listing brings more calls than the website. The two work best together: the profile gets you found, and the website convinces people to contact you.

## Step by step: create your profile {#setup}

1. **Search for your business first.** On Google Maps, search your business name. If a listing already exists, claim it instead of creating a duplicate. Two listings for one business hurt both.
2. **Go to business.google.com and add your business.** Use a Google account you will keep long term, ideally your business email.
3. **Pick your primary category carefully.** It is one of the strongest relevance signals. Choose what you are (“Website designer”, “Dentist”, “Bakery”), not a keyword list. Add a few secondary categories.
4. **Add your location or service area.** If customers visit you, add the exact address. If you go to them, hide the address and list the areas you serve.
5. **Add one phone number and your website.** Use the same number and business name as on your website. Consistency matters.
6. **Verify.** Google chooses the method for your business: commonly a short video of your premises and signage, sometimes phone, email or a postcard. Follow its instructions exactly.

## What helps you show up in “near me” searches {#rank}

| Factor | What Google looks at | What you can do |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Relevance | How well your profile matches the search | Right categories, full services list, clear description |
| Distance | How close you are to the searcher | Accurate address or service areas (you can’t fake this) |
| Prominence | How well known and trusted you are | Reviews, photos, a strong website, mentions on other sites |
Table: Based on Google’s published explanation of how local results are ranked.

## Fill in everything {#complete}

- **Services:** list each service with a one-line description.
- **Hours:** regular and holiday hours. Wrong hours lose calls and trust.
- **Photos:** storefront, interior, team and work, added regularly.
- **Description:** what you do, for whom and where, in plain words. No phone numbers or links.
- **Posts:** an offer, project or update every week or two keeps the profile active.
- **WhatsApp / chat:** add your WhatsApp link so people can message you from the listing.

## Reviews: the part you control most {#reviews}

Ask every happy customer, right after the job, with your direct review link (Profile → Ask for reviews). Reply to every review, good or bad, within a few days. Never buy reviews or offer rewards for them: it breaks Google’s rules and can get reviews removed or the profile suspended.

## Common mistakes {#mistakes}

- Keywords stuffed into the business name (“WebAlchemy Best Web Designer Delhi”). It is against the guidelines and risks suspension.
- Different phone numbers on the profile and the website.
- Duplicate listings for the same business.
- A website link that goes to a slow or broken page.
- Setting it up once and never touching it again.

```faq
heading: Google Maps listing: common questions
Q: Is a Google Business Profile free?
A: Yes. Creating and managing a Google Business Profile is free. Google Ads are a separate, paid product.

Q: How long does verification take?
A: It depends on the method Google chooses. Video and phone verification can be reviewed within days; a postcard can take a couple of weeks. Edits after verification are usually reviewed within minutes to a few days.

Q: Can I list my business without a shop or office?
A: Yes. Service-area businesses can hide their address and list the areas they serve instead.

Q: Why is my business not showing on Google Maps?
A: Common reasons are an unverified or suspended profile, a category that doesn’t match the search, being far from the searcher, or few reviews compared with competitors nearby.

Q: Do I need a website if I have a Google Business Profile?
A: The profile gets you found; the website convinces people. A fast website with clear services and prices also strengthens your profile’s prominence.
```
