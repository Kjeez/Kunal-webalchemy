Short answer: before booking, patients check **who the doctor is, whether you treat their problem, where you are and when you're open, what it might cost, and how easy it is to book**. If your website answers those five things on a phone in under a minute, it brings appointments. If it doesn't, they tap the next clinic on Google.

We have built websites for dental clinics in Gurugram, eye hospitals in Noida, physiotherapists, Ayurveda and homeopathy practices. The same questions come up every time. Here they are, in roughly the order a patient asks them.

## 1. "Can this clinic treat my problem?" {#treat}

Patients rarely search for a clinic name. They search for a problem or a treatment: "root canal in Gurgaon", "cataract surgery Noida", "physiotherapist for back pain near me". If your site has one "Services" page listing twenty treatments, it ranks for none of them and answers none of them.

- **Give each main treatment its own page.** What it is, who needs it, how many visits it takes, recovery time and what to expect.
- **Use the words patients use.** "Teeth cleaning" next to "scaling", "lazy eye" next to "amblyopia".
- **Answer the scary questions.** "Does it hurt?", "How long will I be off work?" These are what patients actually worry about.

## 2. "Who is the doctor?" {#doctor}

People choose a doctor, not a building. A clinic website without the doctor's photo and qualifications feels anonymous, and anonymous feels risky.

- **Photo, name, qualifications and registration** for every doctor.
- **Years of practice and areas of focus**, in plain language.
- **A short personal note** on how they work with patients. It doesn't need to be long; it needs to sound like a real person.

## 3. "Is it close, and are they open now?" {#location}

Most clinic searches happen on a phone, often the same day. The patient wants to know if they can get there and if someone will pick up.

- **Address, Google Map and directions** with landmarks and parking.
- **Timings on every page**, including Sundays and holidays.
- **The same phone number everywhere**: website, Google Business Profile and signboard. Mismatched numbers lose calls and confuse Google.

## 4. "What will it cost?" {#cost}

Patients are nervous about bills they can't predict. You don't have to publish a full price list, but silence makes people assume the worst.

- **Consultation fee**, stated clearly.
- **"Starting from" prices** for common treatments, with a note that the final cost depends on examination.
- **Payment options**: UPI, cards, EMI and any insurance or cashless tie-ups.

## 5. "Can I trust this place?" {#trust}

Patients look for signs that the clinic is real, clean and professional.

- **Real photos of the clinic**: reception, treatment rooms, equipment. Stock photos of smiling models do the opposite.
- **Hygiene and sterilisation practices**, explained briefly.
- **Your Google reviews**, linked or embedded, so patients see real feedback where it lives.

Keep medical content factual. Avoid promises of results or "guaranteed cure" claims, and check the advertising rules that apply to your practice before you publish.

## 6. "How do I book?" {#booking}

This is where most clinic websites lose the patient. They have decided to come, and then they can't find how.

- **A WhatsApp button and a call button** fixed at the bottom of the screen on mobile.
- **A three-field appointment form**: name, phone, preferred time. Not ten fields.
- **A reply promise**, like "We confirm your slot on WhatsApp within an hour during clinic timings", and then keep it.

## 7. "What happens at the first visit?" {#first-visit}

Uncertainty stops people from booking, especially for dental work, physiotherapy or anything new to them.

- **A short "Your first visit" section**: how long it takes, what to bring (reports, prescriptions, ID), and whether they'll get treatment the same day.
- **Answers to common questions** on each treatment page.

## 8. "Does the website work on my phone?" {#mobile}

A slow or broken site makes a clinic look careless, which is the opposite of what patients want from a doctor.

- **It should load fast on mobile data.** Test it on your own phone, away from office Wi-Fi.
- **Text readable without zooming, buttons easy to tap.**
- **No pop-ups covering the page** the moment it opens.

## 9. "Is this clinic up to date?" {#up-to-date}

Old timings, a doctor who left two years ago or a "Diwali offer" from last year all make patients wonder what else is out of date.

- **Update timings, doctors and fees** whenever they change.
- **Keep the copyright year and contact details current.**
- **If you post health tips, keep them recent**, or don't add a blog at all.

## A quick checklist for your clinic website {#checklist}

| Patient question | What your site needs | Quick test |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Can you treat my problem? | A page per main treatment | Search "[treatment] in [your area]" |
| Who is the doctor? | Photo, qualifications, registration | Is the doctor on the homepage? |
| Close and open now? | Map, timings, one phone number | Find your timings in 5 seconds on mobile |
| What will it cost? | Consultation fee, starting prices | Is any price on the site? |
| Can I trust you? | Real clinic photos, Google reviews | Are any photos stock images? |
| How do I book? | WhatsApp, call, short form | Book yourself, from your phone |
| First visit? | What to expect and bring | Is it written anywhere? |
| Works on my phone? | Fast, readable, no pop-ups | Load it on mobile data |
| Up to date? | Current doctors, timings, fees | Read your own site today |
